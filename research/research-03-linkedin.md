# LinkedIn Recruiter: Search, Ranking, and Profile Fields

Research date: 2026-09-01. Sources marked by tier: **(A) LinkedIn official help/product docs**, **(B) LinkedIn Engineering blog / published research papers**, **(C) recruiter-practitioner reports**, **(D) SEO/content-marketing sites**. Tier D claims are flagged and treated as unverified unless corroborated by A or B.

## 1. Recruiter search mechanics, Spotlights, Recommended Matches, result ordering

**Architecture.** LinkedIn Recruiter search runs on the Galene search stack: a federated architecture where a broker (`seas-federated-search`) distributes a query to sharded index partitions, each partition retrieves and scores matching profiles with an ML model, and a federator re-ranks the merged results with additional "dynamic" (near-real-time) features (B: [Search Federation Architecture at LinkedIn](https://engineering.linkedin.com/blog/2018/03/search-federation-architecture-at-linkedin), Mar 2018; [Galene search & relevance infra](https://engineering.linkedin.com/search/tech-talk-galene-search-relevance-infrastructure)).

**Ranking (Recruiter-specific).** LinkedIn Engineering's own writeup, "AI Behind LinkedIn Recruiter Search and Recommendation Systems" (B, [linkedin.com/blog/engineering](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/recommendations/ai-behind-linkedin-recruiter-search-and-recommendation-systems), Apr 22 2019, by Qi Guo, Sahin Cem Geyik, Cagri Ozcaglar, Ketan Thakkar, Nadeem Anjum, Krishnaram Kenthapadi), states results are ranked by three named factors:
- Similarity of the candidate's work experience/skills to the search criteria
- Job posting location
- **Predicted likelihood of a positive response** ("InMail Accept") to recruiter outreach

Ranking uses Gradient Boosted Decision Trees with a **pairwise learning-to-rank objective** that compares candidates within the same search context, later augmented with neural embeddings (LINE-style network embeddings) for semantic title/skill similarity, GLMix models for per-recruiter/per-contract personalization, and multi-armed bandits for session-level exploration. The optimization target is explicitly **InMail Accept rate**, not just retrieval relevance — i.e., ranking is response-probability-driven, not pure keyword match.

**Spotlights (verified against the live help page, last updated ~4 weeks before this research date).** The current official list (A: [Spotlights in Recruiter and Jobs, LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a414283)) differs from older/community terminology. Current names and verbatim definitions:

| Spotlight | Definition (verbatim from LinkedIn Help) |
|---|---|
| Open to work | "Highlights candidates who match your search criteria and have indicated that they're open to hearing from recruiters." |
| Active talent | "Helps you uncover candidates who are more active on LinkedIn and more likely to respond to Recruiter outreach based on their profile activity." |
| Have company connections | "Identifies candidates who are 1st-degree connections of employees at your organization." |
| Interested in your company | "Highlights candidates who selected 'I'm interested' in future roles at your company or interacted with your company's content." |
| Rediscovered candidates | "Helps you easily spot candidates your team has previously interacted with that match your existing search criteria." |
| Internal candidates | Match your criteria, currently work at your org, in-role ≥ 6 months. |
| Candidates saved by | Sourced by Hiring Assistant or manually by a recruiter. |
| Applicants | Applied to the job post in your project. |
| Missed candidates | Uncontacted for longer than a week. |
| Verifications | Have profile verifications (workplace, school, government ID). |

Note: the terms in the task brief ("More likely to respond," "Engaged with talent brand," "Past applicant," "Company alumni") map to LinkedIn's **current** naming as "Active talent," "Interested in your company," "Applicants"/"Rediscovered candidates," and "Have company connections" respectively — LinkedIn has renamed these Spotlights over time (a WebSearch snippet of the same URL from a slightly earlier crawl still showed the old names, confirming a recent rename). Recruiter Lite gets only "Have company connections" and "Interested in your company."

**Recommended Matches** (A: [LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a413241)) is a separate, AI-driven discovery surface, distinct from ranked search results. It combines three signal families: (1) the recruiter's own hiring signals (save/hide/message = positive/negative feedback), (2) job-post signals (title, location, required skills), (3) member job-seeking signals (Open to Work, "more likely to change jobs"). It updates live as the recruiter acts and LinkedIn states it surfaces up to ~10% additional qualified candidates beyond standard search — this percentage is LinkedIn's own marketing claim, not independently verified.

**Overall ordering:** searched results are not pure keyword-match lists; they are a response-probability-weighted rank blended with match relevance and location, refined per-recruiter (GLMix), with Spotlight tags overlaid as filters/badges rather than a separate ranking signal.

## 2. LinkedIn Engineering research: architecture details

Three primary artifacts, all confirmed via direct fetch/search of the papers:

- **"Towards Deep and Representation Learning for Talent Search at LinkedIn"** (B, [arXiv:1809.06473](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.06473), submitted Sep 17 2018, accepted CIKM 2018). Moves LinkedIn Talent Search from linear/GBDT models toward deep models that learn dense embeddings for sparse entity IDs — recruiter IDs, candidate IDs, and skill-entity IDs — built on Economic Graph data, then apply learning-to-rank on top of these representations. The paper frames its contribution as generalizable to "any multi-faceted search engine."
- **"Talent Search and Recommendation Systems at LinkedIn: Practical Challenges and Lessons Learned"** (B, [arXiv:1809.06481](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.06481)) — a companion practitioner paper (found via search, not independently fetched in full this session — treat its details below as titles/abstract-level only pending direct verification).
- **"Learning to Retrieve for Job Matching"** (B, [arXiv:2402.13435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13435), submitted Feb 21 2024, KDD 2024). This is about **job search/recommendation for job seekers**, not Recruiter's candidate search — worth distinguishing from the candidate-ranking papers above. Two retrieval paths: (a) for *promoted* jobs, a graph built from confirmed-hire data scores a seeker's qualification for a job and uses learned graph edges for retrieval (explainable/debuggable by design); (b) for *organic* jobs, personalized dense embeddings trained for seeker engagement, refined by rules mined from categorized member feedback. Infra: a custom GPU solution supporting both approximate-KNN and term matching, alongside a conventional inverted index.
- **"Building Representative Talent Search at LinkedIn"** (B, [engineering.linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/hiring/building-representative-talent-search-at-linkedin), Oct 10 2018, Geyik & Kenthapadi). Describes a post-processing re-ranking layer that partitions candidates by inferred gender, ranks within group by the ML score, then interleaves groups to match the qualified-candidate-pool's demographic distribution at every result position — an explicit fairness intervention layered on top of the relevance/response-probability ranker. **This feature ("Representative Results") has since been sunset**, per a notice on the live page; LinkedIn now frames this work under broader "Responsible AI" principles rather than a shipped feature.
- **"AI Behind LinkedIn Recruiter Search"** engineering post (cited in §1) is the most Recruiter-specific and current of the group.

Common thread across all: candidate-side features are dominated by **structured entities** (canonical title, canonical skill, company, member ID) rather than raw free text, embedded via network/graph-based representation learning, then combined with response-likelihood modeling.

## 3. Profile sections and retrieval weight

Retrieval in Galene is field-indexed: a search matches structured fields (title, skill, company, school, location) plus free text. What's LinkedIn-confirmed vs. inferred:

- **Headline, Experience titles, Skills, Company, Location, Education** — **LinkedIn-confirmed as indexed/searchable fields**: the Recruiter engineering post explicitly lists "canonical titles and skills," "company names," and location as core matching/ranking features (B, source in §1). Location additionally gates result inclusion via the workplace-type filters (A, §5).
- **Skills section mechanics** — **LinkedIn-confirmed structurally**: profiles support up to 50 skills (some sources report a raised cap to 100 in 2024, unverified against an official page this session — treat as **tier C/D, unconfirmed**), and members can pin their top 3 skills, which display prominently while the rest sit behind "Show more." That the *top-3 pin* materially changes search ranking weight (vs. just profile display) is **not stated in any LinkedIn official source found**; it is a **tier D inference** repeated across SEO blogs.
- **Endorsements** — **Tier D, unverified.** Widely repeated stats ("17x more profile views," "50+ endorsements outrank 0 endorsements," "5+ skills = 27x more recruiter searches") appear only on SEO/content-marketing sites with no traceable LinkedIn primary source. No LinkedIn Engineering post or Help page found in this research states endorsement count is a ranking signal. Treat as folklore, not fact.
- **Skill Assessment badges** — **LinkedIn-confirmed for two things, not a third.** (1) Official Help copy (A: [linkedin.com/help/.../a507663](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507663)) states a badge "signals to other members and recruiters that you've taken initiative... can help us surface more relevant content or opportunities for you" — a soft, non-quantified claim. (2) The Engineering post on assessments (B: [Building Blocks of LinkedIn Skill Assessments](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/skills-graph/the-building-blocks-of-linkedin-skill-assessments), Sep 17 2019, Christian Mathiesen & Jie Zhang) confirms passed-skill data **feeds the Galene search stack and Jobs matching**, that **Recruiter search can filter on verified skills**, and cites an early-results figure of **"~30% improvement in likelihood to hear back from a recruiter"** for job-seekers who pass assessments — a LinkedIn-reported (not independently audited) internal metric. Neither source claims assessments boost generic keyword-search rank; the confirmed effect is filterability + response-likelihood association, which is a meaningfully narrower claim than "ranking boost."
- **About/Summary, Experience descriptions, Certifications, Featured, Recommendations, Industry** — **no LinkedIn-official or Engineering-blog statement found** confirming these as indexed/weighted retrieval fields specifically for Recruiter search. Practitioner (tier C) consensus treats About and Experience descriptions as contributing free-text keyword matches (plausible given the platform is a search index over profile text) but this is **inferred, not confirmed**, in this research pass.
- **Open To preferences (job titles, location types, employment type)** — **LinkedIn-confirmed** as both a Spotlight input and a hard filter input (Workplace types filter), see §1 and §5.

## 4. Open To Work: recruiter-only vs. public #OpenToWork

Per official Help (A: [linkedin.com/help/.../a507508](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507508)):
- **"All LinkedIn members"** setting adds the public green #OpenToWork photo frame, visible to your network and in feed/search, and is visible to recruiters too.
- **"Recruiters only"** setting shows a private "Open to Work" note **inside Recruiter's own search UI only** — no photo frame, not visible to regular members, only visible to accounts using LinkedIn Recruiter.
- LinkedIn states it "takes steps to prevent LinkedIn Recruiter users who work at your current company from seeing your shared career interests" using the "I am currently working here" flag, but explicitly **cannot guarantee complete privacy**.
- For India-based users specifically, recruiters see notice period/availability and expected salary regardless of the visibility setting chosen (A, same source).
- **No published, LinkedIn-sourced quantitative study was found in this session on inbound-message delta between public #OpenToWork vs. recruiter-only vs. off.** The earlier-cited "64% higher InMail response with Spotlights + search" figure (tier A marketing copy, [LinkedIn Talent blog](https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/product-tips/spotlight-candidates-on-linkedin-who-are-open-to-new-opportunities)) measures Spotlight usage broadly, not the Open to Work signal specifically, and is a LinkedIn-published marketing stat rather than an audited study — treat with appropriate skepticism despite being tier A/company-sourced.

## 5. Location, remote preferences, cross-border search

Per official Help (A: [Search by location in Recruiter](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a417339); [Filter by workplace type](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a527316)):
- A location selection carries an **implied 100-mile radius**; recruiters can narrow via postal code.
- Location filters support Country, Metro Area, City granularity — **city-level search is not supported for all countries** (relevant: unclear from this research whether Brazil is fully supported at city granularity — not observable from the fetched page; would need a direct check inside a live Recruiter seat).
- **Workplace type interacts with location differently per type**:
  - **Onsite**: candidates within the 100-mile radius (current residence or stated relocation willingness).
  - **Hybrid**: candidates open to relocating to the state/country level, or within the 100-mile radius.
  - **Remote**: **location is disregarded entirely — results are global.**
- Candidates can be filtered as "current location only," "open to relocate only," or both.
- The **Workplace types filter** only returns results among candidates who have set **Open to Work** preferences — i.e., a Brazil-based candidate who wants to surface for "Remote" searches from US/EU recruiters should (a) set Open to Work (recruiter-only is sufficient — it doesn't need to be public), (b) set workplace-type preference to Remote (and/or Hybrid) inside Open to Work settings, and (c) list a location, since Remote search bypasses location entirely but the candidate's Open-to-Work workplace-type and job-title/location preferences are what the filter actually keys on. This is the direct mechanical implication of the two official Help pages above, not a separate claim.
- No LinkedIn source found in this session addresses **time-zone-based filtering** as a native Recruiter mechanic; the "time zone compatibility" framing that surfaced in search results was from a **tier D/C source** (hireflow.net) and is not corroborated by any LinkedIn Help page — treat as speculative/practitioner heuristic, not a documented filter.

## 6. Skill assessments, certifications, badges: effect on ranking

Already detailed in §3. Summary: **LinkedIn-confirmed** — badges are Recruiter-filterable and feed the Galene index/Jobs matching pipeline; LinkedIn self-reports a ~30% response-rate lift for job-seekers who pass and display them (self-reported internal metric, not third-party audited). **Not confirmed** — no source states assessments move a candidate up in *ranked-order* search results (as opposed to being filterable, or improving job-match relevance signals that feed response-likelihood scoring, which indirectly could affect rank given the response-likelihood ranking factor from §1). Certifications and other LinkedIn-native badges (verifications: workplace, school, government ID) are confirmed as a **Spotlight-visible attribute** ("Verifications" Spotlight, §1) but no ranking-weight claim was found for them.

## 7. Other measurable signals

- **Profile completeness / "All-Star."** LinkedIn's own onboarding UI ties All-Star status to a checklist (photo, headline, location, current position + description, ≥2 past positions, education, ≥5 skills, ≥50 connections) — this checklist structure is widely reported but **the specific criteria list above was not independently re-verified against a current LinkedIn Help page in this session** (sourced from aggregated search-result text, tier C/D). The oft-repeated "40x more likely to appear in search" figure is a **very old LinkedIn statistic** (originally circa 2013–2014, no primary source found or re-confirmed here) that content sites keep recycling without a live citation — **treat as unverifiable folklore**, not a current fact.
- **Social Selling Index (SSI).** LinkedIn does not present SSI as a Recruiter ranking input; search results explicitly note "LinkedIn Recruiter does not present the SSI as a criterion for ranking candidates" (tier C summary, no official LinkedIn statement located either confirming or denying this directly in this session). SSI is being deprecated/restricted to Sales Navigator for most users per a Sep 2026-adjacent secondary source (tier C/D, [duperrin.com](https://www.duperrin.com/english/2026/08/17/end-linkedin-ssi/), Aug 17 2026) — **not independently verified against an official LinkedIn announcement**; flag as needing confirmation before relying on it.
- **Connection degree (1st/2nd/3rd).** No LinkedIn Engineering or Help source found in this session states connection degree is a Recruiter search-ranking feature. Community claims that "you rank higher for recruiters who are 2nd-degree vs 3rd-degree" are **tier D, unverified** — plausible given personalized-search precedent elsewhere on LinkedIn (People You May Know, feed ranking use network proximity, per B: [Building a Large-Scale Recommendation System: People You May Know](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/recommendations/building-a-large-scale-recommendation-system-people-you-may-know)), but not confirmed specifically for Recruiter's candidate-search ranker.
- **Activity/engagement recency.** **LinkedIn-confirmed indirectly**: the "Active talent" Spotlight (§1) explicitly keys on "more active on LinkedIn... based on profile activity," and the Recruiter engineering post's response-likelihood ranking factor plausibly incorporates recency-correlated signals, though the exact feature set is not published field-by-field.

## Bottom line for a Brazil-based candidate targeting international remote roles

Mechanically confirmed levers, in order of directness: (1) set Open to Work with workplace type = Remote/Hybrid and desired locations/titles listed (recruiter-only is enough, avoids employer visibility risk) — this is what the Workplace-types Remote filter actually keys on, since Remote search ignores profile location; (2) keep canonical job titles and skills in Experience/Skills fields matching how recruiters phrase searches, since these are the confirmed indexed/ranked fields; (3) passing relevant Skill Assessments is confirmed to make you filterable and is associated (LinkedIn's own self-reported figure) with better response rates. Everything about endorsement counts, pinned-skill algorithmic weight, All-Star's exact search multiplier, SSI, and connection-degree ranking effects is **not confirmed by any LinkedIn primary source found in this research** — it is recycled SEO folklore (tier D) and should not be treated as fact when making profile-optimization decisions.
